# Q3 evidence on very distant protein recognition

**Foundation models can recover distant protein groups missed by conventional comparisons.** The clearest evidence comes from experiments that supplied known protein domains. From whole reporter DNA, the strongest conventional method matched the strongest model at the same query budget. The tested layouts show how candidate selection and protein boundaries can change the comparison. Evidence across different reporter ancestries within each layout remains sparse. [Research scope](SCOPE.md)

## What the benchmarks measured

Sequence identity is the fraction of aligned positions with the same amino acid. A low whole-protein identity can coexist with one shorter region that provides enough evidence for a correct match. Q3 therefore needs to count actual correct identifications on cases the conventional methods miss, along with the identity and how much of a protein the match covers. The two Wittmann studies motivated evaluating divergent examples first and including strong conventional methods. The project used independently documented natural proteins and public reporter records. Its endpoint is correct annotation in the tested reference library. [Science, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adu8578) · [Frontiers, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2026.1858951)

| Input to the comparison | Main low-identity evidence | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known protein domain, predict its structural superfamily | 128 domains below 25% global identity, grouped into five folds and 52 held-out fine families. Competing labels share the query's broad fold. | Local ESM-2 150M ranked 99 correctly. Raw alignment and the stronger HMM profile each ranked 86 correctly. The model recovered 8 of the 17 cases missed by all four conventional comparators. |
| Known intact enzyme, predict its exact experimentally documented reaction | 22 proteins below 30% identity to every available same-reaction reference, including 14 below 25%. | Raw alignment and local ESM-2 150M got the same 19 right. The reaction profile got 18 right. Below 25%, all three got 11 of 14 right. |
| Whole DNA, distant fluorescent and Renilla reporter families | Seven eligible reporter occurrences below 25% across two ancestries, assessed with their respective fixed libraries. | At a cap of two, gene calling delivered six covering inputs. Raw alignment, HMM profiles and local ESM-2 correctly identified the same six. All three identified all seven when supplied the documented proteins. |
| Whole DNA, the GUS plant reporter in four archived layouts | Seven records in four submission groups from one ancestry. All are near 17% identity to their best same-group reference. | Both preparations reached all seven at one query per record. Raw alignment and ESM-2 8M each recovered seven. The HMM profile and ESM-2 150M each recovered zero on this library. |

[Five-fold study](https://github.com/khgng026/iri-construct-screening-2026/pull/28) · [Distant reporter study](../../experiments/paired_preprocessing/DISTANT_REPORTERS.md) · [GUS study](../../experiments/paired_preprocessing/Q3_GUS_CONTEXT.md)

In the five-fold study, weighting the fold groups equally gave local ESM-2 150M 83.5% accuracy and the stronger HMM 70.3%. The paired difference was +13.1 percentage points, with a descriptive whole-fold interval of −3.1 to +27.5 points. The model led in three folds. A conventional method led in each of the other two. The correct model calls on conventional misses span four folds. This supports complementarity on the tested domains and leaves the average gain on new folds uncertain. Global identity ranged from 11.8% to 24.6% in the primary group. The study has only four observations in its next identity tier, so it provides too little evidence to estimate a general identity crossover. [Five-fold protocol and decisions](https://github.com/khgng026/iri-construct-screening-2026/pull/28)

With a wider reference set and the same 128 domains, the model got 98 right, the strongest profile got 85, and raw alignment got 78. Six individual model recoveries on conventional misses repeated across both reference settings. Each method was choosing among two to four labels. In the small groups of conventional misses, uniform guessing would average about six to seven correct calls, so the rate at which the model can rescue such cases remains uncertain. The model's advantage across the full low-identity panel is the stronger result. [Reference-depth comparison](https://github.com/khgng026/iri-construct-screening-2026/pull/28)

## What changed across construct layouts

The GUS result held in a compact single reporter, a GFP–GUS fusion, a long dual-reporter series, and a long single-reporter vector. Six records carry the same GUS domain. On that isolated domain, ESM-2 8M identified the correct structural group and all three conventional comparators missed it. This counts as one rescue of a distinct sequence. Raw alignment recovered the same calls as ESM-2 8M when it received the full product or fusion candidate. The resulting parity on whole DNA held in every tested GUS layout. [GUS cases and decisions](../../experiments/paired_preprocessing/Q3_GUS_CONTEXT.md)

The larger grouped vector cohort gives more context on layout. At two queries, Pyrodigal improved recall over minimal conversion by about 13–14 percentage points on backbone-heavy examples and 9 points on multi-CDS examples. The fusion estimate was uncertain. Within the cohort's broader below-40% tier, normalized alignment was 8 points ahead of mean-pooled ESM-2 on the same gene-caller inputs. That tier and model configuration differ from the primary below-25% comparisons. The groups were also concentrated in a few component families. [Grouped vector cohort](../../experiments/paired_preprocessing/COHORT_RESULTS.md)

The earlier same-cohort reference ablation suggested a large model advantage below 30%. It used legacy alignment-derived labels and no profile comparator. The independent-label results here show that the strongest method can change with the structural family, reference set, model size, and whether a scorer receives a complete product or a domain alone. These factors explain why one identity cutoff provides an incomplete summary of Q3. [Exploratory cohort ablation](../../experiments/paired_preprocessing/COHORT_SCORER_FOLLOWUP.md)

## Where to resume

1. To measure an end-to-end model advantage, select a fresh, prespecified group of archived benign reporter or model-organism housekeeping constructs with independent labels and several ancestries per layout. Retain more than one natural reference library. Count rescues and losses on the same candidates alongside primary recall. Use the whole product and verified domain as separate diagnostics. Weight related copies together.
2. Keep raw alignment and HMM profiles in the comparison. Record whether each documented component was reached under the same query cap, score identical selected proteins across methods, and report residues and compute. The existing five-point criterion applies to the preprocessing comparison in Q2. Prespecify the practical effect and uncertainty for a future Q3 scorer comparison.
3. Treat the reported superfamily calls as broad assignments. Exact function has a separate, smaller benchmark. Pretraining overlap is unknown, and coverage of independently verified ancestries across layouts limits the construct result. A later study should use distinct sources and an independently held-out panel for stronger population estimates.
